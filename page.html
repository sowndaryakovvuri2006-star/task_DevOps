Radha and Krishna are central figures in Hindu mythology and are often worshipped together as the embodiment of divine love and devotion. Krishna is considered the eighth avatar of the god Vishnu and is famous for his playful, loving nature and his role as a protector of dharma (righteousness). Radha, on the other hand, is Krishna’s beloved consort and is often seen as the personification of pure devotion (bhakti). Their relationship is not just romantic but symbolizes the soul's longing and love for the divine. The stories of Radha and Krishna are celebrated in many classical texts, poetry, music, and dance forms, especially in the Bhakti movement. Their divine love transcends the material world, teaching spiritual values of love, devotion, and unity with God.
